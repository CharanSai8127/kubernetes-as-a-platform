# What Is Chaos Engineering

Chaos engineering is the practice of:
- intentionally introducing controlled failures into systems

to validate:
- resilience
- recovery behavior
- and operational survivability.

The goal is not:
- creating random outages.

The goal is:
- understanding how distributed systems behave during failures.

---

# Why Chaos Engineering Exists

Failures are inevitable in distributed systems.

Examples include:
- pod crashes
- node failures
- DNS instability
- dependency saturation
- storage failures
- and network partitions.

Chaos engineering validates whether:
- the platform survives these failures safely.

---

# The Core Principle

Reliable systems are not systems that avoid failure.

Reliable systems are systems that:
- survive failure predictably.

---

# Controlled Failure Injection

Failures are introduced intentionally to observe:
- recovery behavior
- blast radius
- dependency amplification
- and operational stability.

This helps identify:
- hidden weaknesses
before:
- production incidents occur naturally.

---

# Key Insight

Chaos engineering validates resilience under failure,
not perfection during normal operation.
