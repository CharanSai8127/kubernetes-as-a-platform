# Predictive Scaling

Reactive autoscaling responds after:
- operational pressure already becomes visible.

Predictive scaling attempts to prepare capacity before instability begins.

---

# Why Predictive Scaling Exists

Distributed systems require time for:
- startup
- readiness
- infrastructure provisioning
- and traffic convergence

during scaling events.

Reactive scaling therefore creates:
- temporary overload windows.

Predictive scaling attempts to reduce these delays.

---

# Predictive Signals

Predictive systems may use:
- historical traffic
- scheduled demand
- workload trends
- business patterns
- or forecasting models

to anticipate future operational pressure.

---

# Operational Benefits

Predictive scaling improves:
- responsiveness
- startup resilience
- and latency stability

during expected workload spikes.

This reduces:
- delayed scaling behavior
- and overload propagation.

---

# Operational Tradeoffs

Predictive scaling may increase:
- infrastructure cost
- spare capacity
- and operational complexity

if forecasts become inaccurate.

Incorrect prediction may create:
- wasted resources
- or insufficient scaling.

---

# Platform Perspective

Modern platforms increasingly combine:
- reactive scaling
- predictive scaling
- and workload-aware autoscaling

to improve operational reliability.

---

# Key Insight

Predictive scaling attempts to preserve stability before reactive autoscaling fully observes operational pressure.
