# Overprovisioning Risks

Overprovisioning means:
- allocating more infrastructure capacity than workloads actually require.

Distributed systems commonly overprovision because of:
- survivability fear
- inaccurate forecasting
- and oversized resource requests.

---

# Why Overprovisioning Matters

Unused infrastructure still generates:
- recurring cloud cost.

Examples include:
- oversized nodes
- unnecessary replicas
- and excessive failover capacity.

---

# Runtime Infrastructure Behavior

Overprovisioned systems continuously consume:
- compute
- memory
- storage
- and networking resources unnecessarily.

---

# Operational Challenges

Improper overprovisioning may create:
- infrastructure sprawl
- poor utilization
- and financially inefficient platforms.

---

# Platform Engineering Perspective

Reliability without efficiency becomes:
- economically unsustainable.

---

# Key Insight

Excess survivability without infrastructure efficiency amplifies operational economics.
