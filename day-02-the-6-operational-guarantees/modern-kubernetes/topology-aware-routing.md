# Topology-Aware Routing

Topology-aware routing improves traffic locality awareness across distributed infrastructure.

Traditional service routing may distribute traffic across:
- zones
- nodes
- or regions

without considering:
- infrastructure proximity
- latency
- or traffic locality

Topology-aware routing helps preserve efficient workload communication under growing scale.

---

# Operational Goal

Topology-aware routing exists to:
- reduce unnecessary cross-zone traffic
- improve latency stability
- and preserve infrastructure efficiency

Distributed systems become more stable when traffic remains closer to workload locality.

---

# Traffic Locality

Kubernetes can prefer routing traffic toward:
- nearby workloads
- local zones
- or infrastructure-adjacent endpoints

This improves:
- response latency
- infrastructure efficiency
- and workload coordination

under large-scale traffic conditions.

---

# Infrastructure Efficiency

Reducing unnecessary cross-zone communication helps:
- decrease network overhead
- reduce infrastructure cost
- and improve workload performance predictability

Topology awareness becomes increasingly important as:
- cluster size
- traffic volume
- and infrastructure distribution

continue growing.

---

# Failure Isolation

Traffic locality also improves:
- fault-domain isolation
- workload resilience
- and infrastructure containment

Failures affecting one zone or region become less likely to destabilize unrelated workload communication paths.

---

# Kubernetes Evolution

Topology-aware routing reflects Kubernetes evolution toward:
- infrastructure-aware scheduling
- workload locality optimization
- and distributed-system efficiency engineering

Modern Kubernetes increasingly coordinates:
- traffic behavior
- workload placement
- and infrastructure topology

as integrated platform concerns.

---

# Key Insight

Topology-aware routing improves distributed-system stability by preserving traffic locality across infrastructure boundaries.
