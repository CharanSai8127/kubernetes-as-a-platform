# Rollout and Recovery

Kubernetes continuously coordinates deployment transitions and workload recovery behavior.

Rollout and recovery mechanisms help preserve:
- workload continuity
- deployment stability
- operational reversibility
- and controlled recovery coordination

during changing application and infrastructure conditions.

---

# Rolling Updates

Kubernetes performs deployment transitions gradually through rolling updates.

Controlled rollout progression helps:
- preserve workload availability
- reduce deployment blast radius
- and maintain operational continuity

during application changes.

Deployment safety depends heavily on coordinated workload replacement behavior.

---

# ReplicaSets and Revision History

ReplicaSets preserve:
- deployment history
- previous workload revisions
- and rollback coordination

Revision tracking improves:
- deployment traceability
- recovery reliability
- and operational visibility

Reliable rollback depends on preserving trusted deployment history continuously.

# Rollback Coordination

Kubernetes supports rollback through:
- deployment revision restoration

Rollback helps restore:
- previously stable workload behavior

- and deployment continuity

after unstable release transitions.

# Progressive Deployment Behavior

Controlled rollout progression may include:
- staged deployment
- traffic shifting
- canary rollout
- and gradual operational validation

Progressive deployment reduces:
- large-scale outage amplification
- unstable traffic redistribution
- and widespread deployment failure propagation

Distributed systems become safer when operational change occurs gradually.

# Recovery Stability

- deterministic deployment behavior
- trusted desired-state history
- and controlled reconciliation coordination

- operational uncertainty
- deployment inconsistency
- and manual intervention

during outage conditions.

Rollout and recovery mechanisms help prevent:
